# Pink Noise for Sleep

Pink noise sounds like steady rainfall: softer than white noise, with the low frequencies a little louder than the high ones. Many listeners pick it when white noise feels too harsh and brown noise feels too deep.

AuraFlow pink noise is generated live in your browser — no recording, no loop, no restart. Type "pink noise" at [auraflowfocus.com](https://auraflowfocus.com) and hear it built live, free to try with no account.

## What you'll hear

An even, rainfall-like wash that stays consistent in character without ever repeating the same audio. It masks sudden household sounds without adding a hiss that draws attention to itself.

## Pink noise vs white noise vs brown noise

White noise: bright, even hiss, all frequencies equal. Pink noise: the same idea, tilted toward the low end, so it sounds softer and more like rain. Brown noise: tilted further still, a deep rumble. Try all three free — the right one is usually the one you stop noticing fastest. The [full noise colors guide](../white-noise-vs-brown-noise-vs-pink-noise/) breaks each one down.

### What is the difference between white noise, brown noise, and pink noise?

White noise contains all audible frequencies at equal intensity — a bright, steady hiss. Pink noise tilts toward the lower frequencies, sounding softer, like steady rainfall. Brown noise tilts further still, producing a deep, warm rumble. All three mask sudden background sounds; which one suits you is usually the one you stop noticing fastest, and all three are free to compare.
